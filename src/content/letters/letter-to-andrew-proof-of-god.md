Dear Andrew,

Whether God is really there, not just whether believing in him makes people feel better, is the right question. I wrote you once before about dominoes and minds and death. This time I want to go a layer deeper and give you a set of tools: what "proof" really means, several separate lines of evidence, a test you can run with your own life, and plain answers to the three objections you'll hear most.

## Part 1: Proof is not the same as knowledge

People often say, "You can't prove God exists." Before you answer, ask them what they mean by "prove."

In math, a proof means you start with things everyone grants and reason step by step to a conclusion that can't be false. That kind of proof is rare. You can't prove in that way that your mother loves you, that George Washington lived, or that the bridge you drive over will hold. But you **know** all those things.

Knowledge is simpler and more common than proof. To know something is to be able to represent it as it really is, on an appropriate basis of thought and experience. You know your friend's voice on the phone. You know the stove is hot. You know it from evidence, from experience, and from reasoning that fits together. That's how most knowledge works, and it's how knowledge of God works too.

> Knowledge and faith are intended to go together.
>
> — The Allure of Gentleness, ch. 4

> Faith is not opposed to knowledge; it is opposed to sight
>
> — In Search of Guidance

So the honest question isn't, "Can you give me a math proof?" It's, "Is there good reason to believe God is real, better reason than to believe he isn't?" I think there is, and that reasons from several directions all point the same way. That's what a good detective looks for. One clue can mislead. Many clues pointing the same way is how cases get solved.

## Part 2: Everything leans on something

Last time I used dominoes. Let me come at it from a different angle: leaning.

Look at anything around you, a tree, your hand, the sun. Ask: **does this thing explain its own existence?** The tree needs soil, water, sunlight, and a seed. The sun needs hydrogen and gravity and laws of physics that hold steady. Nothing physical carries the reason for its being inside itself. Everything leans on something else.

Now imagine a long row of books leaning on each other on a shelf. Each book is held up by the next one. Add more books, a thousand, a million, an endless row. Does adding more leaning books ever explain why the whole row stands? No. A row of things that all lean needs something that doesn't lean, a bookend.

> The dependent character of all physical states, together with the completeness of the series… logically implies at least one self-existent, and therefore non-physical, state of being
>
> — "Language, Being, God, and the Three Stages of Theistic Evidence" (essay)

That essay has three stages, and they're worth knowing. Stage one: there must be something self-existent that everything else depends on. Stage two: that something must be like a mind, a great mind, because of what we find in the world it produced. Stage three: we look at history, especially Jesus, to find out whether that mind has made himself known. The first stage alone doesn't give you the God of the Bible. It gives you a starting point that the other stages fill in.

Paul pointed the same way: "Ever since the creation of the world his eternal power and divine nature, invisible though they are, have been understood and seen through the things he has made" (Romans 1:20).

## Part 3: Truths that aren't made of anything

Here's a line of evidence many people never notice.

You know that 7 + 5 = 12. You know that if all dogs are mammals and Rex is a dog, then Rex is a mammal. You know that torturing a child for fun is wrong. Ask about each one: **what is it made of?** Not atoms. Not energy. You can't find "7 + 5 = 12" anywhere in space. It wasn't true only after people invented counting. It was true before the first star.

> …the laws of logic have no factual import. They would remain true if no minds existed
>
> — "A Realist Analysis of the Relationship Between Logic and Experience" (paper)

So reality contains things that are real but not physical. That alone breaks the claim that only matter exists. And look how strange it is that our minds can grasp these truths. A pile of molecules shaped by survival alone gives you no reason to trust its reasoning about abstract truth. But a mind made by a greater Mind would fit that ability exactly.

> If narrow Naturalism cannot provide for truth, it also cannot provide for logical relations
>
> — "Knowledge and Naturalism" (paper)

Here's a test you can use. When someone argues that only physical things are real, notice that they're using logic, truth, and good reasoning to make the case. Those aren't physical things. Their argument depends on the very things it says don't exist.

## Part 4: Order points to a mind

You've seen a sand castle on a beach. You've also seen piles of sand. You never wonder whether the castle was made by waves. Order of a certain kind, especially order that serves a purpose, shows the work of a mind.

The universe is full of that order. The laws of physics are stable and can be written in mathematics. The constants are balanced so finely that small changes would make stars, chemistry, and life impossible. Living cells carry information in DNA, like a written code.

> Relative to our data, then, we must believe that the first cause of physical reality would also be a mind, and a sufficiently great mind at that.
>
> — The Allure of Gentleness, ch. 4

Someone will say science explains this order. But science describes how order behaves. It doesn't explain why there's order to describe.

> …any sort of evolution of order of any kind will always presuppose pre-existing order
>
> — "Language, Being, God, and the Three Stages of Theistic Evidence" (essay)

"The heavens are telling the glory of God; and the firmament proclaims his handiwork" (Psalm 19:1).

## Part 5: The law inside you

Now look inward. You have a conscience. You know some things are really wrong, not just unpopular. When someone cheats you, you don't say, "Well, that's just his preference." You say, "That isn't fair." You're appealing to a standard that stands over both of you.

Every culture has disagreed about many details, but none has admired cowardice, betrayal of friends, or cruelty for fun. Paul noticed this: "They show that what the law requires is written on their hearts, to which their own conscience also bears witness" (Romans 2:15).

Where does a real moral law come from? A law that binds every person, everywhere, can't come from atoms, and it can't come from votes, because majorities have been terribly wrong. It fits best with a good and personal source, someone to whom we're actually answerable. Your conscience is a small window onto that.

Here's the plain argument. If there is no God, real moral obligation is very hard to explain. But real moral obligation exists. You know it does every time you say "that's not fair" and mean it. So that's another clue pointing the same way.

## Part 6: Test it by living

Arguments are like a map. A map is useful, but at some point you have to walk the trail. Jesus offered exactly that kind of test: "Anyone who resolves to do the will of God will know whether the teaching is from God or whether I am speaking on my own" (John 7:17).

Notice the order. He doesn't say, "Feel certain first, then obey." He says, "Resolve to do it, and you'll know." It's an experiment. God isn't an object in a lab that sits still while you look at it. He's a person, and persons are known by interacting with them. You come to know a friend by talking, listening, trusting, and seeing what happens.

> …faith is never meant to exist apart from knowledge, where knowledge is possible.
>
> — Living in Christ's Presence

So here's what an honest experiment looks like for you:

- For a month, pray every day, honestly, even if it's just, "God, if you're there, show me."
- Read one chapter of the Gospel of Mark each day. It has sixteen.
- Pick one thing Jesus clearly taught, like forgiving someone or telling the truth when it costs you, and actually do it.
- Write down what you notice: answered prayers, changes in you, moments that seem more than coincidence.

God says, "When you search for me, you will find me; if you seek me with all your heart" (Jeremiah 29:13). That's a promise you can check.

## Part 7: The evidence of Jesus

The first parts of this letter tell you there's a great, good, non-physical mind behind everything. The question then is whether that mind has spoken. Christianity makes a claim you can actually investigate: Jesus rose from the dead in real history.

About twenty-five years after the crucifixion, Paul wrote down a summary he had been given much earlier: "that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the scriptures, and that he was buried, and that he was raised on the third day in accordance with the scriptures, and that he appeared to Cephas, then to the twelve. Then he appeared to more than five hundred brothers and sisters at one time, most of whom are still alive" (1 Corinthians 15:3–6). "Most of whom are still alive" means: go ask them.

Consider a few facts most historians, including skeptical ones, accept. Jesus was crucified. His followers were scattered and afraid. Soon afterward they became convinced they had seen him alive, and they said so publicly in the very city where he died. Many suffered for it, and none of them gained money or power from it. People will die for something they believe is true. They don't die for something they know they made up.

Paul even made this the hinge of the whole thing: "if Christ has not been raised, then our proclamation has been in vain and your faith has been in vain" (1 Corinthians 15:14). Christianity is not afraid of the question. It puts its weight right there.

## Part 8: What arguments can and can't do

Let me be honest with you, because you deserve that.

- Arguments can show that believing in God is **reasonable**, more reasonable than not believing.
- They can clear away bad reasons for unbelief.
- They can't force anyone to believe. A person who doesn't want God to be there can always find another question.
- They can't replace knowing God personally. They bring you to the door. Walking through is a different act.

That's not a weakness. It's how God made it. He wants children who love him freely, not prisoners of a syllogism.

## Part 9: The three biggest objections

### "If God is good, why is there so much evil?"

This is the hardest one, and it should be. Here are three things to hold. First, real love requires real freedom, and real freedom means people can choose terribly. A world of robots would have no evil and no love. Second, our complaint against evil assumes a real standard of good. If there's no God, "evil" is just "things I don't like." The problem of evil actually leans on a moral law. Third, Christianity doesn't explain suffering from a distance. It says God entered it, on a cross, and promises a day when "he will wipe every tear from their eyes. Death will be no more" (Revelation 21:4). You won't have every answer. But God is not hiding from the question.

### "Science has replaced God."

Science studies how the physical world works, and it's wonderful. But it can't tell you why there's a physical world at all, why it follows laws, or why the laws can be understood by minds. Many of the founders of modern science believed in God precisely because they expected an orderly universe from an orderly Mind. Science and faith answer different questions. Don't let anyone make you choose.

### "Then who made God?"

The argument never said everything needs a maker. It said things that **depend** on something else need an explanation. God, by definition, is the one who doesn't depend on anything. Asking who made him is like asking what's north of the North Pole. The question doesn't fit the thing.

## A last word

Andrew, you don't have to turn off your mind to follow Jesus. You'll need all of it. God invites honest thinking: "Come now, let us argue it out, says the Lord" (Isaiah 1:18). Keep asking. Keep testing. Keep living what you already know is true. The evidence will keep piling up, and one day you'll realize that the God you were reasoning about has been with you the whole time, closer than your next breath. As Paul said, "he is not far from each one of us. For 'In him we live and move and have our being'" (Acts 17:27–28).

With love, and with great respect for your mind,

Dallas
