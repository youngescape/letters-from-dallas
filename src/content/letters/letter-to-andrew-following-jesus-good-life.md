Dear Andrew,

I've written you about big questions: whether God is there, what he's like, what happens when we die. Now I want to write about the question that matters every single day: how do you actually live? Not in theory. On a Tuesday, at school, with your phone in your pocket and your friends around you. I want to give you something concrete you can build your life on, and a plan you can start this week.

## Part 1: What the good life really is

Most people think the good life means having good things: money, a great job, a nice car, lots of followers, a girlfriend, a fun weekend. Those can be fine. But you've probably already noticed that people who have plenty of them are often anxious, bored, or unkind.

Here's a better way to say it. The good life isn't mainly about what you **have**. It's about who you **become**. A good life is the life of a good person, someone whose heart is so full of love and wisdom that doing what is right becomes natural, even easy. Think of the kindest, steadiest, most joyful adult you know. That person has something money can't buy.

Jesus said, "I came that they may have life, and have it abundantly" (John 10:10). Abundant life is not a full calendar. It's a full heart: "love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, generosity, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control" (Galatians 5:22–23). Read that list again and ask yourself honestly: would you want to be that kind of person? Would people want to be around you? That's the good life.

And here's the surprise. Becoming that person isn't something you do by gritting your teeth. It's something that happens when you live close to Jesus and learn from him.

## Part 2: Following Jesus means being his apprentice

The word "disciple" sounds old and religious. A better word today is **apprentice**. An apprentice is someone who stays close to a master so he can learn to do what the master does. A young carpenter watches, practices, makes mistakes, and slowly gets good at the craft.

Jesus' invitation was simple: "Follow me" (Matthew 4:19). He also said, "A disciple is not above the teacher, but everyone who is fully qualified will be like the teacher" (Luke 6:40).

So what are you learning from Jesus? Not just facts about him. You're learning how to live your life the way he would live it if he were you. How would Jesus be a 15-year-old in your school? How would he handle a friend who betrays him, a test he didn't study for, a group chat that turns ugly, a temptation late at night? That's the curriculum.

> Jesus shows his apprentices how to live in the light of the fact that they will never stop living
>
> — The Divine Conspiracy, ch. 3

Being his apprentice doesn't mean being heavy and serious all the time. Jesus said, "Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me; for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light" (Matthew 11:29–30). A yoke is a harness that lets two animals pull together. You're not pulling alone. You're pulling alongside him.

## Part 3: The kingdom is available now

Jesus' main message was this: "The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has come near; repent, and believe in the good news" (Mark 1:15).

What is the kingdom of God? It's simply the range of God's effective will, where what God wants actually happens. Your "kingdom" is everything you have say over: your room, your words, your choices. God's kingdom is where his goodness is in charge. And Jesus says it has come near, it's right here, within reach.

> The eternal life that begins with confidence in Jesus is a life in his present kingdom, now on earth and available to all. So the message of and about him is specifically a gospel for our life now, not just for dying.
>
> — The Divine Conspiracy, Introduction

> Eternity is not something waiting to happen, something that will commence later. It is now here.
>
> — The Divine Conspiracy, ch. 8

That means the Christian life isn't mostly about waiting to get into heaven after you die. It's about letting heaven into your life now. Many churches have shrunk the gospel down to "how to get your sins forgiven so you can go to heaven." I've called those "gospels of sin management." Forgiveness is wonderful and you need it. But Jesus offers far more: a new kind of life, starting today.

## Part 4: The VIM pattern

How does anyone actually change? Not by wishing. Every real change in a person's life follows a simple pattern. I call it VIM: **Vision**, **Intention**, **Means**.

Think about learning to play guitar.

- **Vision:** You see someone play and think, "I want to do that." You have a picture of what's possible.
- **Intention:** You decide. Not "maybe someday," but "I'm going to learn this."
- **Means:** You get a guitar, find lessons, and practice scales every day, even when it's boring.

Without vision, you never start. Without intention, you never commit. Without means, you never get anywhere.

Following Jesus works the same way.

- **Vision:** See how good life in God's kingdom is. Read the Gospels and watch Jesus. Notice how free, loving, and unafraid he was. Let it make you hungry.
- **Intention:** Decide to become his apprentice. Say it to him plainly: "Jesus, I want to learn to live like you. I'm in."
- **Means:** Use practical activities that actually train you. Those are called spiritual disciplines.

## Part 5: The disciplines, explained simply

A spiritual discipline is something you do that you can do, so that over time you become able to do what you can't do yet by direct effort.

Here's an example. You can't make yourself stop being anxious just by trying hard in the moment. But you can practice being quiet with God for ten minutes each morning. Over months, that practice changes you, so that when stress comes, you're steadier. It's like an athlete. A basketball player can't decide during the game to suddenly have better free throws. He makes them in the game because he practiced hundreds of them when no one was watching.

Paul said, "Train yourself in godliness, for, while physical training is of some value, godliness is valuable in every way, holding promise for both the present life and the life to come" (1 Timothy 4:7–8).

Here are the disciplines I'd start with, sized for you.

### Solitude and silence

Be alone with God, without noise. Jesus did this: "In the morning, while it was still very dark, he got up and went out to a deserted place, and there he prayed" (Mark 1:35). **For you:** ten minutes a day, phone in another room. Sit outside or in your room. You don't have to say anything. Just be with God. "Be still, and know that I am God!" (Psalm 46:10).

### Fasting from your phone

Fasting means going without something good for a while so you learn that God is enough. **For you:** one evening a week with no phone or screens after dinner. Or keep your phone out of your bedroom at night. Notice how often you reach for it. Each time, turn that reach into a short prayer.

### Scripture memorization

What fills your mind shapes your life. "I treasure your word in my heart, so that I may not sin against you" (Psalm 119:11). **For you:** learn one short passage a month. Start with Matthew 11:28–30 or Psalm 23. Write it on a card, read it before bed, say it while you walk. Then use it when you're tempted or worried.

### Prayer

Prayer is talking with God about what you and he are doing together. **For you:** pray in plain words, wherever you are. Before a test. Walking to class. When someone annoys you. Jesus said, "whenever you pray, go into your room and shut the door and pray to your Father who is in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you" (Matthew 6:6).

### Service

Jesus "came not to be served but to serve" (Mark 10:45). **For you:** do something useful at home without being asked. Help a younger kid. Stack the chairs. Serving trains you out of thinking the world revolves around you.

### Secret acts of kindness

Do good and don't let anyone find out. "But when you give alms, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing, so that your alms may be done in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you" (Matthew 6:3–4). **For you:** once a week, do something kind that nobody will know was you. Leave an encouraging note. Pay for someone's lunch. Then tell no one. This frees you from needing people's approval.

## Part 6: Grace is not opposed to effort

Now an important warning. None of this earns God's love. You already have it, all of it. But that doesn't mean you do nothing.

> Grace is not opposed to effort, it is opposed to earning. Earning is an attitude. Effort is an action.
>
> — The Great Omission

Earning says, "If I do enough, God will love me." Effort says, "God loves me, so I'm going to train to live like his Son." Paul puts both together: "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; for it is God who is at work in you, enabling you both to will and to work for his good pleasure" (Philippians 2:12–13). You work. God works in your working. Grace actually gets used up fastest by the people who work hardest at becoming like Jesus, because they keep running into how much they need it.

So when you miss a day, don't panic, and don't quit. Just start again tomorrow. That's grace.

## Part 7: A simple weekly plan

Here's a plan you can actually do. Don't try to be a monk. Start small and keep it going.

**Every day**
- Morning: two minutes before you look at your phone. Say, "Jesus, I'm your apprentice today. Teach me."
- Read one short passage from the Gospels. Start with Mark, a little each day.
- Night: phone out of your bedroom. Look back on the day and ask, "Where did I see God? Where did I miss him?"

**Three times a week**
- Ten minutes of silence and solitude.
- Work on your memory verse.

**Once a week**
- One secret act of kindness.
- One evening without screens.
- Go to church, and talk with your dad or a friend about something you're learning.

**Once a month**
- A longer time alone with God, maybe a long walk or an hour outside.
- Look back: what's changing?

Try it for eight weeks. Then adjust it. It's a tool, not a test.

## Part 8: What changes over time

Here's what you can expect, Andrew, honestly.

At first, not much will seem to happen. Disciplines can feel awkward or even boring, like practicing scales. Keep going.

After a few months you'll start to notice small things. You get angry a little less fast. You're less worried about what people think. A verse comes to mind right when you need it. You find yourself praying without deciding to.

Over years, you become a different kind of person. Things that were hard become easy. Things that used to tempt you lose their grip. You start loving people you used to avoid. "And all of us... are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another" (2 Corinthians 3:18). That's what the good life is: not a single moment but a slow, steady becoming.

And the best part is that you'll never stop. You're an unceasing spiritual being, and the life you build with Jesus now continues forever. Nothing you learn with him is wasted.

Jesus said, "Everyone then who hears these words of mine and acts on them will be like a wise man who built his house on rock" (Matthew 7:24). That's my prayer for you. Not just that you'd know these things, but that you'd do them, and build a life that holds up in every storm.

## A blessing for you

Andrew, may you see how good the life of Jesus is, and want it. May you decide to be his apprentice. May you find simple daily ways to stay close to him. And may you grow, year by year, into a person full of love, joy, and peace, who makes everyone around you glad you're there.

With love, and cheering you on,

Dallas
